## Interpretation, limitations and decision

The two tested native objectives differ in **required successful actions** and publicly declared missions, but they share MiniGrid movement/observation physics, and both policies are trained from a **handwritten public teacher**. This is not generic transfer across arbitrary mechanics, autonomous goal inference, or lifelong task-independent learning. The hand-engineered front-cell/hashed mission contract, pickup-evidence bit, four model seeds and fixed demonstration distribution limit generality. Replay's dramatic source retention is consistent with rehearsing the otherwise overwritten old target, but equal cached-example counts do not isolate every neural mechanism or imply unbounded retention. The target success rate remains low (24/128 scratch and naive; replay 28/128); teacher training coverage is not a full solution. Workshop and XLand remain diagnostics and are not substitutes for the native physical-goal witness.

**Next useful preregistered question:** independently verify old-task retention/replay when target mechanics change further (for example, Farama Fetch/PutNear requiring actual pickup/drop and nonvacuous changes) and compare simple public action-history or maintained recurrent baseline with an information-matched reactive control. Screen any pre-satisfied target layout *before* measuring rewards. Use entirely NEW source/target final seeds, new model seeds and actual native actions; don't tune/reopen M15–M18 independent finals. Reuse maintained PyTorch/SB3-Contrib before inventing an optimizer. The experiment should test retention at matched target updates/extra-label exposure while reporting task-specific physical effects, target learning, and failure interpretation.
